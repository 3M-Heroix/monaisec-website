How AI agent failures can affect financial operations

Synthetic experiment

Duplicate payment after a lost acknowledgement

A payment service can lose the response after a provider accepts an instruction. In a MONAISEC simulation, an unguarded resubmission could create a second transfer. A recovery policy that checks the provider after the retry window avoided duplicates in the tested cases and sometimes left the status unresolved.

Read the payment-agent recovery study

Documented automation incident

Unexpected orders can create unwanted positions

In 2012, an error in Knight Capital’s automated order router generated millions of orders in 45 minutes, according to the SEC. The incident involved conventional trading software. For agent-driven trading, it motivates checking whether order limits and suspension controls operate independently of the agent.

Analytical scenario

Dependence on the same AI provider

The Financial Stability Board identifies third-party dependency and provider concentration as potential vulnerabilities. In a MONAISEC scenario, an outage at a shared provider could interrupt several institutions’ agent workflows at once, delaying time-sensitive operations where fallback capacity is limited.

Methodology, scope & limitations

This is a curated initial analytical register, not a quantitative risk assessment, stress test, incident database or legal opinion. It distinguishes (1) vulnerabilities explicitly identified in official publications, (2) established financial transmission channels, and (3) MONAISEC agent-specific analytical pathways. The presence of an official source does not mean that the source validates every agent scenario in the record. Consequences are conditional and depend on deployment context, autonomy, permissions, scale, market conditions, provider concentration, substitutability and controls. Related regulatory IDs are navigation cross-references, not legal determinations of applicability.
