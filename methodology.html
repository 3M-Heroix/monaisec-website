Methodology & evidence standards.

How MONAISEC collects, classifies, connects and reviews evidence about AI-agent security and financial-system risk.

Version 1.0.0Reviewed 08 Nov 2026Primary-source first
Research boundary: MONAISEC is an independent research initiative. Its analysis is not legal, investment, supervisory, audit or operational advice. Curated coverage is incomplete and does not imply endorsement by cited organisations.

Core research principles

Principle 1

Primary-source first

Prefer legislation, regulator publications, standards-body documents, official incident disclosures and original research. Secondary reporting is contextual and must not replace the underlying source where accessible.

Principle 2

Separate fact from analysis

Label what the source explicitly states separately from MONAISEC interpretation, threat modelling and scenario construction. Never present analytical extrapolation as a documented event.

Principle 3

Jurisdiction and applicability

Record jurisdiction, issuer, legal status, effective dates, covered actors and scope. Applicability is context-dependent; no single global strictness score is used.

Principle 4

Evidence maturity

Use consistent evidence labels and retain provenance. A framework taxonomy is evidence of a recognised risk pattern, not proof that a particular financial institution experienced it.

Principle 5

Traceable relationships

Graph edges and cross-register links must identify whether they are directly stated by a source or analytically mapped by MONAISEC.

Principle 6

Versioned research

Keep stable record IDs, ISO dates, version numbers and review notes. Corrections should be traceable; never silently rewrite the historical basis of a claim.

Evidence classes

Evidence labels describe the type and maturity of support for a claim—not its severity.

Primary source

Official legal text, regulator/supervisor publication, standards-body publication, original dataset or first-party incident disclosure.

Independently corroborated

A claim supported by a primary source and one or more credible independent sources.

Secondary source

Credible analysis or reporting used for context where primary evidence is unavailable or incomplete.

Unverified claim

A claim not yet sufficiently substantiated. Do not use as a factual conclusion.

Analytical hypothesis

A plausible inference, threat pathway or scenario developed by MONAISEC; not an observed fact.

Event and research record types

Observed incident

A real event with credible public evidence. Include date, affected entity (only if publicly confirmed), source, impact evidence and confidence.

Demonstrated attack / proof of concept

A technique shown in a controlled test or research demonstration; not necessarily seen in production.

Known vulnerability / weakness

A disclosed technical weakness, with identifier and affected component where available.

Threat pattern

A recognised attack or failure class from a framework or research taxonomy.

Analytical scenario

A constructed exercise used to explore possible consequences and controls.

Risk assessment and limitations

MONAISEC currently uses qualitative pathways and contextual indicators, not calibrated probability, expected-loss or systemic-risk estimates. Do not interpret labels as quantitative scores.

Review cadence: Review high-impact legal and supervisory changes at least monthly; review technical frameworks and threat intelligence monthly; re-check record URLs and status before external publication or decision use.

Limitations: This is an independent research initiative, not a regulator, law firm, audit firm or financial adviser. Content is not legal, investment, supervisory or operational advice. Coverage is curated and incomplete.

Corrections and research integrity

Corrections should preserve the record ID, identify the field changed, state the reason, cite the supporting source and update the review date. Material corrections should be documented in the changelog. If evidence is withdrawn or a source becomes unavailable, flag the record rather than silently retaining an unsupported conclusion.

Relationship mappings in the Knowledge Graph are analytical unless the underlying source explicitly states the relationship. Scenario Lab examples are constructed exercises unless explicitly marked as observed events with supporting evidence.
