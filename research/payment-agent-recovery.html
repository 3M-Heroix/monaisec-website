Research Pilot 001 · Synthetic experiment

Recovering AI agent payments after a lost acknowledgement

Can a sender recover the original provider transfer ID when an authorised payment’s acknowledgement is lost, without creating a second transfer?

MONAISEC · 20 November 2026 · Pilot v0.3

Results from the simulated provider

The guarded recovery policy created zero duplicate transfers in ten synthetic scenarios. Three scenarios ended with the payment still unresolved. Each of the two comparison cases produced a duplicate after the first transfer had already been accepted.

Retry and lookup

A retry with the original idempotency key inside the simulated retention period returned the original transfer ID if the provider had already accepted the payment. After the automatic retry window closed, an available matching lookup also recovered the ID.

Unresolved outcomes

Two cases with an unavailable lookup looked identical to the sender. In one, the provider had accepted the payment. In the other, it had never received the request. The third unresolved case returned no matching record. The sender made no new submission in these three cases.

Comparison cases

After a lost acknowledgement, generating a new key or reusing the original key after its simulated expiry each created a second transfer in the corresponding comparison case.

Local rollback check

When the outbox write failed, the local payment entry and approval consumption rolled back together. After the fault was removed, retrying the approval created one local payment entry and one outbound instruction.

Experimental design

Each scripted scenario started with one authorised payment and fresh local SQLite databases for the sender and the simulated provider. The sender committed approval consumption, its local ledger entry and a durable outbound instruction together. The harness checked transfer counts against the provider ledger. The sender saw only submission responses and the results of its configured lookup.

The simulated provider retained idempotency keys for 60 logical ticks. The guarded policy retried with the original key through tick 44. From tick 45, it checked for a matching provider transfer and held the instruction for review when the outcome remained unclear. The two comparison cases changed the key or extended retry past the retention boundary. Two local reruns of the same fixtures reproduced the recorded outcomes using Python 3.12.14 and SQLite 3.53.1.

Scope and limitations

The test used scripted payment instructions, sequential dispatch and a local provider lookup. Its scope ends at simulated provider acceptance and sender reconciliation. Live payment providers, concurrent dispatch, network behaviour, LLM performance and settlement remain outside the test. The counts describe these fixtures. The retry and retention windows are simulation parameters.

A follow-up experiment could delay when an accepted payment appears in the provider’s lookup and measure the time it takes to reconcile the instruction, alongside the number of duplicate transfers.
